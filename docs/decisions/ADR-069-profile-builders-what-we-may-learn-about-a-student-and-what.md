# ADR-069 · Profile builders: what we may learn about a student, and what each is allowed to influence

## Decision

| Builder | What it tells us | May influence | May never influence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **OCEAN** (existing app) | Personality | Tone and phrasing; which environments are mentioned (cohort size, structure) | Which options are shown, their order, eligibility |
| **Interests (RIASEC / Holland codes)** | Interest profile | Which fields appear, especially wildcards in the deck | Eligibility, ranking verdicts |
| **Values sort** (Schwartz-style) | Life values | The life-value weights (ADR-068) | Facts |
| **Tasters** (work-sample style micro-tasks from a field) | Felt fit with a field | Field suggestions; the student's own notes | Anything automatic |
| **Situational prompts** | Readiness, how they handle situations | Tone and support content | Eligibility, ranking |
| **Skills self-assessment** | Skills vs what a route needs | Readiness view (ADR-050), learning tracks | Eligibility verdicts |

## Rules

- Every builder is optional and says what it will be used for before the student takes it.
- Results are stored as self-report with their own data state; no claims of psychometric validity.
- Personality may phrase, never rank (standing rule from the vision work).

## Reasoning

Schools and companies learn about people with interest inventories, values sorts, work samples and situational judgement; these give the counsellor a fuller picture without letting a quiz decide a life (Nikhil, 1 Oct).
