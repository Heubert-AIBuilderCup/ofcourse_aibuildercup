# ADR-078 · Route dimensions are facts or preferences with their impact shown; coins are for life values only

## Decision

- **The six route dimensions are not coin-weighted.** Each is asked by its nature, and every answer immediately shows what it does:

  | Dimension | Asked as | Impact shown on answer (example) |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Cost | Fact: budget per year (chip from CRM or typed), fees only / fees and living | "36k a year: Perth and Geelong within budget; Sydney about 4k over" |
  | Time | Fact: how soon; most years you'd give it | "Starting Feb: 3 intakes open; the 1-year master's fits" |
  | PR pathway | Preference: stay after — yes / not sure / no | "Yes: courses off the occupation list seal; regional study gains points" |
  | Work rights & experience | Preference: need to earn while studying? | "Yes: city campuses rise; placements count" |
  | Location | Preference: city / suburban / regional; community or relatives nearby matters? | "Regional: more PR points, cheaper rent, fewer part-time jobs" |
  | Career prospects | Preference: career-focused or academic; target field if known | "Career: courses with placements and a strong local job market rise" |
  | Family | Fact: dependants | "Partner and child: dependant costs added; partner work rights matter" |

  The engine derives its weights from these answers; the student can still adjust them on the weights plaque (ADR-030) and ask "what if I were flexible on…" (ADR-060).
- **Coins are for life values only** (ADR-068, 073), framed as what they are: *"These ten coins are your attention, time and energy for the next few years. Where do they go?"* across health, social life, financial life, learning and growth, closeness to family, community and relatives nearby, independence, adventure. Max 5 per pile, all placed, piles start empty, "not sure" = even spread marked as an assumption, one line of effect per coin. It is the **last, skippable step of the opening** ("spend them later" from the profile).

## Reasoning

Spreading coins across facts (budget, timing) and preferences (where to live) asks a student to express a budget as attention, which makes no sense (Nikhil, 1 Oct). Facts need hard data; preferences need a choice and its consequences; only values are a scarce budget to allocate.

## Supersedes

ADR-072. The opening becomes six screens, one kind of action each: 1 Facts about you (where you are now; budget with hard-limit-or-roughly, fees only or with living, who pays; timing; dependants; confirm qualifications) → 2 What you'd study (particular / a few areas / suggest me; level; hands-on or classroom; length) → 3 Where and how you'd live (destination; city / suburban / regional; community or relatives nearby; climate optional) → 4 After you graduate (stay after; career or academic; earn while studying; settle or return) → 5 Three futures with assumption chips → 6 Life-values coins, skippable.
