# ADR-045 · Design principle: it should feel like a game — seamless, not gamified

## Decision

The Studio borrows what games do well and refuses what habit apps do. Adopted:

- **Teach by doing:** the first Ask question is the tutorial; no walkthrough.
- **Consequence feedback:** every answer produces a named, visible reaction ("Curtin noticed: cost matters to you").
- **Fog of war and reveal:** the overview fills in as the student explores; progress is the map, not a bar.
- **Gates as bosses with known strategies:** "not yet reachable" always comes with the ways past, shown as moves.
- **Checkpoints:** ranked routes are saves; autosave everything; undo everywhere; no confirmation modals.
- **A companion, not a narrator:** the duck guides and reacts; authority comes from source lines.
- **Juice:** small, fast, physical responses to every touch; cards deal, tiles settle, numbers count up.
- **One continuous surface:** shared-element transitions; the tile becomes the card; nothing pops in from nowhere.

Refused: streaks, points, badges, leaderboards, fake difficulty. A course is sealed only for a true reason, always shown.

## Reasoning

- Games are the best-known way to teach without a manual, show consequences, and keep people going through hard choices, which are exactly the Studio's problems (Nikhil: "it should feel like a game, seamless").
- Gamification mechanics would trivialise a decision about someone's life and would erode the trust the source lines build.

## Constrains

A design-review checklist for every Studio screen. Applies to the admin app only in "juice" and "one continuous surface"; the admin app is a tool, not a game.
