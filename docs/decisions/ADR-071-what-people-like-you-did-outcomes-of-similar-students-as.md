# ADR-071 · "What people like you did": outcomes of similar students, as evidence

## Decision

- Route detail, Compare and both briefs show **outcomes of similar students**: acceptance rate, visa grant rate, completion, which courses they most often took, where they ended up (job, post-study visa, PR), drawn from Expert's own application and visa history and, later, from portal outcome check-ins (ADR-050, with consent).
- **"Similar"** is defined on a few profile features: passport country, grades band, English band, field, budget band, destination. The definition is shown ("students from Nepal with 65–70% and IELTS 6.5 applying to regional campuses").
- Every figure carries **sample size, time window and data state**; shown to students only above a **minimum group size (proposed: 20)** and never for a group small enough to identify anyone.
- **Outcomes only, never popularity.** No "most students chose…"; that remains excluded (ADR-049).

## Reasoning

Knowing how students like you actually fared is the most grounding information a counsellor can give, and Expert holds the data (Nikhil, 1 Oct). It is evidence about consequences, not pressure toward the crowd.

## Constrains

A cohort-outcomes read model over CRM applications/visa outcomes (de-identified) with the minimum-group rule enforced in the query. Framed as information with a source line, not advice. Hackathon: a static block on Priya's ECU route and in the briefs with mock figures and a visible sample size.
